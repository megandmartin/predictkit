# Frame an opportunity

Use for discovery research and product definition. Work from supplied content; no browsing or interviews are available to this worker. Founder statements are hypotheses unless supporting observations are included.

## Find the decision, not just the market
Connect the named person → recurring situation → current workaround → costly decision → information a forecast might add. Use the actual question and alternative in the brief. A generic “prediction market for everyone” is not a user job.

Ask whether uncertainty is the real bottleneck. If the answer is already observable, a source lookup may be more useful. If users need preference aggregation, a poll may suffice. A forecast is useful when uncertain future outcomes influence an action before resolution. Explain the comparison without silently replacing the founder's chosen project.

Distinguish three propositions: users face this problem; they will record or use forecasts; the resulting information improves a decision. Evidence for one does not establish the others. Name one counterexample user or situation where the proposed flow is unnecessary.

## Make the next evidence useful
Choose the assumption whose failure most changes the product. Design a small test with a relevant participant profile, a realistic task, observable behavior, a proposed success/failure criterion, and the decision each result would trigger. Prefer past behavior and actual task completion to “would you use this?” answers.

Useful interview prompts ask about the last time the person made this decision, what they used, what happened, and the cost of being wrong. Do not present proposed interviews, recruitment, thresholds, or expected results as performed or measured.

## Handoff
Give one focused user journey, a success event the app can expose, the strongest difference hypothesis, and unresolved questions ordered by their effect on scope. Keep quantitative claims absent unless supplied evidence supports them. A coherent opportunity is a reason to test, not proof of demand.

This skill supplies instructions only. The role returns its assigned artifacts; it cannot recruit users, execute tests, or access other concurrent discovery jobs.
